1. Executive summary

This project examines India's e-commerce market and develops an initial market-sizing view for online electronics and appliances. The analysis uses published market benchmarks, descriptive visualisation and transparent growth scenarios. A structured questionnaire and a consent-gated clustering workflow are included as the basis for a later consumer study.

2. Problem statement and objectives

Research question: How do category preference, price sensitivity and purchase frequency vary among Indian online consumers, and what do these patterns imply for the addressable market and growth potential of online electronics and appliances?

  1. Describe the published trajectory of India's overall e-commerce market.
  2. Establish a transparent 2024 baseline for the online electronics-and-appliances category.
  3. Estimate illustrative TAM and SAM paths under alternative growth assumptions.
  4. Design a privacy-conscious survey to study purchase frequency, spending bands and purchase-decision factors.
  5. Prepare an exploratory clustering workflow that can be run only after eligible, consented survey responses are collected.

5. Category baseline and TAM / SAM scenarios

The 2024 online electronics-and-appliances estimate of US$27bn is used as the starting TAM assumption. The separate US$39bn smartphone estimate is shown for context only; category definitions may overlap, so the two values are not summed.

Illustrative scenario model

For each scenario, TAM is calculated as 27 × (1 + g)(year − 2024), where g is an assumed annual growth rate. Illustrative SAM is calculated as 30% of scenario TAM. The 30% share is a modelling assumption, not a measured serviceable market.

6. Methodology and analytical workflow

Market analysis

  1. Load the sourced benchmark CSV.
  2. Validate required fields, numeric values and missing data.
  3. Separate reported values from forecasts.
  4. Calculate adjacent-year growth only where calendar years are consecutive.
  5. Generate TAM/SAM scenario and sensitivity tables.
  6. Export reproducible charts, CSV tables and an HTML report.

Consumer research (next empirical stage)

  1. Use the prepared questionnaire and blank response schema.
  2. Obtain voluntary informed consent and collect only broad, non-identifying fields.
  3. Check response completeness, consent and sample adequacy.
  4. Explore purchase frequency, spend bands and price sensitivity.
  5. Run K-Means only on actual eligible survey records; review silhouette scores and cluster profiles.
  6. Describe convenience samples as exploratory, not nationally representative.

Tools

Python, pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib and scikit-learn support the reproducible analysis and optional clustering workflow. An R Markdown notebook and R scripts provide the parallel research notebook and visual analysis.

7. Limitations and responsible interpretation

  • Published market estimates may use different definitions, coverage and estimation methods; they should not be treated as directly interchangeable.
  • Market value / GMV is not necessarily retailer net revenue.
  • The total-market series includes published forecast values; forecasts are not actual observations.
  • The smartphone estimate is kept separate from electronics and appliances to avoid potential overlap and double counting.
  • HCES measures household consumption expenditure, not online purchase transactions.
  • No transaction-level dataset or completed consumer survey is supplied. Therefore, no order-value histogram, measured price elasticity or empirical customer clusters are reported.
  • Illustrative CAGR and SAM reachability assumptions are selected for sensitivity analysis and require external validation.
  • Survey responses, if collected, should be anonymised; names, phone numbers, exact addresses and payment credentials must not be collected or committed.

8. Future scope and conclusion

Planned next steps

  1. Collect a documented, consented exploratory survey sample with coverage across broad age bands, residence types and regions.
  2. Document the sampling frame and avoid national generalisation unless the design supports it.
  3. Acquire permitted transaction data, if available, and document ownership, licence, period and field definitions.
  4. Validate market assumptions against additional authoritative sources and define a specific serviceable customer segment.
  5. Extend the analysis with descriptive consumer profiles and cautious, interpretable segmentation once data quality and sample size permit.

The current project establishes a reproducible baseline for studying India's e-commerce market and the online electronics-and-appliances category. Its main contribution is a transparent separation of sourced market evidence, explicit scenario assumptions and empirical questions that remain open. The next meaningful improvement is real, ethically collected consumer or transaction data—not synthetic records presented as findings.

Reproducibility: Install dependencies with python -m pip install -r python/requirements.txt and run python python/run_project.py from the repository root. The generated charts, analysis tables and runtime HTML report are written under python/reports/.
